## Design Context

### Users

**Primary goal: get hired.** Visitors are recruiters, hiring managers, and engineers doing first-pass screens — often on a shared link or search result, mostly desktop, in a professional evaluation mindset. They need to **trust you quickly** (credible engineer, clear story) and **scan fast** (role, stack, proof). The site must survive a 30-second skim *and* reward a 5-minute deep dive for anyone who advances you.

Secondary: fellow developers and potential collaborators evaluating whether you’re interesting to work with — still professional, not casual social traffic.

### Brand Personality

**Futuristic · Creative · Hacker**

The interface should feel like something built by someone who understands systems at a low level and chose to make something beautiful with that knowledge. Not “hacker” as edgy or aggressive — hacker as *resourceful, curious, building things others haven’t thought of*. Not “futuristic” as generic sci-fi — futuristic as *a plausible near-future operating system that could actually exist*. Creative means unexpected choices and craft, not decoration for its own sake.

**First-impression emotional target:** **confidence** — visitors should read “serious engineer, trustworthy, in control of the craft,” not novelty for novelty’s sake.

### Aesthetic Direction

**xiaoOS — a refined command-center interface.** Keep the OS / HUD concept but push it toward something more *designed* and less *themed*. Prefer a product a design team might ship over a movie prop: **custom-built tool**, not surveillance cosplay.

- **Theme:** Dark by default (professional evaluation contexts; reinforces the OS metaphor). Light mode when offered should feel like a deliberate “day theme,” not a generic white reset.
- **References (spirit, not copy):** Bloomberg Terminal density; the quiet confidence of a well-designed CLI; mission-control UIs that prioritize information over decoration.
- **Anti-references:** Generic developer portfolios (hero + identical project cards + timeline). AI-default aesthetics (gradient text, glass-everywhere, purple–blue neon). “Gamer” energy (noisy chrome, RGB-for-RGB’s sake). Award-site spectacle that hides substance.

**Note:** Older internal docs (e.g. style guides that still mention heavy surveillance framing or decorative gradients) are **legacy** — new design work should follow this file and impeccable bans, not retro surveillance or gradient-text tropes.

### Design Principles

1. **Hiring-first clarity.** Above all, make role, skills, and proof scannable. Wow-factor supports credibility; it never replaces signal.

2. **Functional beauty.** Every visual element should earn its place — status, hierarchy, or OS metaphor. Decorative bits should read as *system details*, not ornaments.

3. **Plausible fiction.** xiaoOS should feel like it *could* be real software: consistent components and copy that sounds like an OS, not a film.

4. **Information density over empty space.** HUD-style density is fine when it carries real information — never clutter.

5. **Refined restraint.** Treat cyan/orange (or successor accents) as semantic: interaction, status, emphasis — not wallpaper. One strong accent beats ten glowing edges.

6. **Earn the wow.** Memorable through coherence and craft — boot flows, transitions, typography — not one-off gimmicks.

### Accessibility & motion

- **Target:** Best-effort accessibility — semantic HTML, visible focus, sensible contrast on text, respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for heavy animation. Not a formal WCAG certification pledge unless you later scope one.

### Constraints (technical)

- Stack as implemented: Go templates, vanilla TypeScript, Tailwind, GSAP/esbuild where used. Favor performance and maintainability; avoid framework churn unless the project moves intentionally.
- **Startup animation:** Keep the existing xiaoOS boot / loader experience (`StartupAnimation` + related markup/CSS). You may refactor styles to tokens or tweak boot **copy** for plausible-OS tone; do not replace the animation wholesale or remove the sequence as the default intro.
